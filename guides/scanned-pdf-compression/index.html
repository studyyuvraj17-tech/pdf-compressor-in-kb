How to Compress a Scanned PDF Without Losing Readability

Scanned PDFs are often larger than text-based PDFs because each page is stored as an image. The goal is not simply to make the file as small as possible: it is to meet the upload limit while preserving the details that matter.

Important: Keep an original copy. Compression results depend on the source PDF, and no target size is guaranteed without possible quality loss.

Why scanned PDFs are harder to shrink

A scan with photographs, colour backgrounds, high resolution or many pages contains more image data. A text-only PDF may shrink substantially with a simple re-save, while a scan may need image compression or a new scan.

Choose colour or grayscale deliberately

If colour carries no useful information, grayscale can reduce size. Keep colour for documents where it distinguishes annotations, stamps, photographs or other meaningful details. Black-and-white thresholding can erase light handwriting or fine marks, so inspect the result.

Use a sensible scan resolution

Very high resolution increases file size, but reducing resolution too far makes small text and fine lines unreadable. If you can rescan the original, test a moderate setting and inspect a sample page before processing the whole document.

Do not over-compress signatures and fine print

After compression, zoom in on names, identification numbers, dates, handwritten entries, stamps, seals and signatures. Look for blur, blocky artefacts, faint lines and clipped edges. Keep the original in case you need to try a less aggressive setting.

Know the trade-off of image-based compression

Some browser tools rebuild pages as images to reach a smaller target. This can make text unselectable and may not preserve links, forms, bookmarks, document structure or accessibility information. If you need searchable text, use OCR or a workflow that preserves text when possible.

Choose a target that matches the portal

Try 100 KB, 200 KB or 500 KB according to the receiving site’s maximum, not an arbitrary smallest size. A very low target may be unsuitable for a long or detailed scan. Check the actual output size and visual quality before uploading.

If it still cannot reach the target

The source may contain too much image detail to fit at acceptable quality. Rescan at an appropriate setting, remove pages only if they are not required, or ask the receiving organization whether another file-size limit is acceptable. Never assume a failed target was achieved.
